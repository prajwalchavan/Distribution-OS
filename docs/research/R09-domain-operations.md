## 1. The channel: primary, secondary, tertiary and the tiers between

| Term           | Meaning                                                        | Who books it                                                                                                      |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Primary sale   | Company (or its C&F / super stockist) invoices the distributor | Company revenue; appears in Tally/SAP of the brand                                                                |
| Secondary sale | Distributor invoices retailer/wholesaler                       | Lives in distributor records; "few [brands] track secondary because it lives in distributor records" (Sortstring) |
| Tertiary sale  | Retailer sells to consumer                                     | Nobody in the chain sees it                                                                                       |

Typical chain: Manufacturer -> C&F agent (state warehouse, no title) -> Super Stockist (buys in bulk, supplies several distributors across a district/cluster; margin cited at 5-7% by Storewise and 8-12% by Arpit Srivastava — conflicting, treat as 5-12%) -> Distributor / Stockist (serves retailers in a town; also called RS = Redistribution Stockist at HUL) -> Sub-stockist / sub-distributor (a smaller distributor buying from a distributor for an outlying area) -> Wholesaler (mandi, very low margin) -> Retailer (kirana, paan, general store) -> Consumer. Kirana Club gives the compressed form "Brand -> Super Stockist (optional) -> Distributor -> Retailer -> Consumer". ~85-90% of mass grocery still moves through traditional trade with 13-14 million kirana stores (Redseer); about 4 lakh distributors are represented by AICPDF (Kotak Insights).

Where Tarsun sits (from the real invoices): a direct distributor for Reliance Consumer (Campa) and Guiltfree (Too Yumm), but a **sub-distributor** for MOM makhana, buying from Guru Kripa Enterprises (a micro-enterprise distributor). This is normal and means the product's "supplier" for a purchase invoice can be a manufacturer, a C&F, a super stockist, or another distributor — the supplier type, not just the brand, decides whether an IRN/QR e-invoice exists on the inbound document.

Primary-vs-secondary tension is the root of several pain points below: brands push primary at month-end ("channel stuffing"), and a widening primary-secondary gap is "almost always an early warning sign: distributor overstock" (MAssist). AICPDF's 2025-26 campaign against Tata Consumer alleged auto-generated "backend orders", stock worth Rs 50-60 crore piled up with Maharashtra distributors, and "when we try to return them, the credit notes fail to generate" (Storyboard18, Financial Express).

## 2. Field sales: beats, PJP, DSR, who employs the salesman, incentives

**Beat** = the ordered list of outlets a salesman covers in one day; **PJP (Permanent Journey Plan)** = the weekly/monthly calendar of which beat is worked on which day, "deliberately stable, typically fixed for three to six months" (SpireStock beat-planning guide). Outlet classes A/B/C drive frequency: "A-class twice weekly, B-class weekly, C-class fortnightly or monthly." Fortnightly packaged foods beats run 40-80 outlets with 6-12 minute calls; daily categories 80-150 outlets. A salesman "walks 8-15 kilometres per day, visits 30-50 retail outlets" (SpireStock attrition article). Real invoice E shows the brand DMS printing "Beat Name: AADHARWADI RUNAK CITY (K-WEST)" and salesman name/phone on the retailer bill — beats are first-class objects in brand DMS data and appear on documents.

**DSR (Daily Sales Report)** — the 10 standard fields (SpireStock DSR guide): date; salesman name/code; beat/route; outlets planned vs visited; productive calls; total order value; collections; returns and damage; new outlets added; remarks/competitor info. Benchmarks quoted (vendor, unverified): productive call rate 60-75% established / 40-55% new territory; lines per call 4-6 in GT; AOV Rs 3,000-8,000 per call for packaged foods; beat adherence 85-95%; monthly collection efficiency 70-85%. KinematicApp gives PCR 55-70% urban GT, 40-50% rural.

**Who employs the salesman.** Company payroll: TSI/SO (Territory Sales Incharge / Sales Officer), ASM. Distributor payroll: DSM / DSR / ISR ("Distributor Salesman"; Haldiram calls them DSM and pays a **DSM incentive "monthly based on secondary sales captured in the DMS", slab-based on target achievement with town-population tiers** — Scribd copy of Haldiram's Dec-2024-Mar-2025 circular). Company-funded "salesman subsidy" arrangements exist but I could not fetch a citable source — mark unverified. This matches the founder's statement that some salesmen are employed by the manufacturer and some by the distributor depending on brand.

**Pay structure.** Entry-level DSM Rs 12,000-18,000/month; AmbitionBox average Rs 15k/month; recommended 60:40 or 70:30 base:variable; top performers at 110%+ target reach Rs 20-22k; attrition 30-50% per year with a "15-25% sales dip for 2-3 months" when a beat changes hands (SpireStock). Incentive parameters seen in practice: per-case incentive for pushing a new SKU ("push a new biscuit SKU for a per-case incentive" — SpireStock scheme engine page), slab payouts by % target achievement (Haldiram), lines-per-call or bill-cut counts, and outlet-billing coverage ("Bill at least 100 of the 110 shops" — MarketingWeekly example). Slab design: incremental vs retroactive (retroactive "lifts the rate on all fifty lakh" once crossed) — RebateLedger.

## 3. Order-to-delivery cycle: pre-sales vs van sales

Two models coexist, often in the same distributor (SpireStock van-vs-pre-sales):

|                       | Pre-sales (order booking / "indent")                                    | Van sales (ready stock / DSD)                                                                |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Who                   | Salesman books order; separate delivery team                            | Driver-salesman sells from vehicle                                                           |
| Order-to-delivery     | "T+1 day typically" — orders consolidated by evening, next-day dispatch | Immediate, same visit                                                                        |
| Categories            | Dairy, bakery, high-value, personal care                                | "impulse items including chips, chocolates, ice cream, soft drinks, biscuits, confectionery" |
| Billing               | Central invoice, printed before dispatch                                | Invoice at outlet on thermal printer                                                         |
| Payment               | Collected at delivery or on next salesman visit                         | Cash/UPI on the spot, or short credit noted                                                  |
| Cost per outlet visit | Rs 14-18                                                                | Rs 22-28                                                                                     |

Tier-2 norm: next-day delivery for booked orders is the default expectation; same-day only for van sales. Kirana delivery windows matter ("before 9 AM (before customer rush)"). A delivery vehicle "carries 50-200 SKUs across 25-40 retail outlets in a single trip"; one case study went from 28 to 36 deliveries per vehicle per day (SpireStock last-mile). A van-sales van "covers 40-80 outlets daily and generates Rs 50,000-2 lakh in same-day sales" (SpireStock van sales). Loading rule: "the last delivery stop is loaded first."

**Van/trip mechanics that the product must model** (Tally guide + SalesOn + Kinematic): morning **load sheet** signed by the driver, warehouse stock moved to a per-vehicle "van godown"; at day end `Stock Loaded - Invoices Billed = Remaining Stock`, extended by SpireStock to `opening stock = sold + remaining + returns`; cash counted against expected collections; saleable returns and damaged goods logged separately on-route; "Undigitized operations lose 3-8% of revenue to various forms of cash-related fraud and errors" (vendor claim). Crew composition: founder says two per vehicle, multiple trips per day depending on return time; sources describe "driver/salesperson" or "delivery boy" but no citable two-man norm — treat as founder-verified operating fact. Delivery boys earn Rs 8,000-12,000 in Tier-2 cities (SpireStock income article). POD options in the market: OTP from retailer, geotagged photo, e-signature; partial delivery generates "a partial invoice matching only the accepted quantities."

**Answer to the CONTEXT open question on van sales:** yes, model it. Snacks, water and biscuits are exactly the van-sales categories, Tarsun already issues a thermal slip for Campa (invoice G), and the delivery app already needs "on-spot orders" — a van-stock ledger (vehicle as a stock location) plus on-vehicle invoicing is the minimum. Recommend treating each trip as a stock-location with load/unload movements in the append-only ledger.

## 4. Units, MRP and the price waterfall

**UoM reality from the real invoices.** Brands sell in cases but invoice in pieces; the case size is embedded in the item name ("x 90", "_120", "(16+5.5)") or in a UOM code ("CS1"); Guru Kripa writes "2 Case" under a 180-pcs line; Guiltfree shows both "QTY (Pcs) 120" and "Boxes 1". Retailers order in pieces or "one box". So each SKU needs a pack hierarchy (piece -> inner/box -> case) with **conversion factor per supplier and per batch/MRP** (brands change case sizes with promo packs like 16+5.5 g). SpireStock's SKU rule is worth adopting: "Never encode anything mutable. Price, supplier, tax rate and MRP all change" — MRP is an attribute of a batch/stock lot, not of the SKU.

**MRP** (Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules 2011): retail packs must declare "Maximum or Max. retail price Rs... inclusive of all taxes", net quantity, month/year of manufacture, manufacturer/packer, consumer care; Rule 18: no dealer may "make any sale of any commodity in packed form at a price exceeding the retail sale price" nor "obliterate, smudge or alter" it; Rule 24: **wholesale packages** (cases) must declare manufacturer, commodity identity and number of retail packages — which is why case counts are printable from the carton. Selling above MRP is an offence; fines "up to Rs 25,000 for a first offence" are quoted by label vendors (indiankanoon copy of Rule 32 shows older Rs 4-5k figures — the Act sets higher penalties; unverified which applies). Practical rule for the product: retailer selling price is capped at MRP; the distributor's rate to retailer is normally expressed as a discount off MRP (retailer margin).

**Price waterfall (industry terms).** MRP -> less retailer margin = **PTR / RLP** (price to retailer, retailer landing price) -> less distributor margin = **PTD / DLP** (price to distributor). Worked example (gyaanokplease): MRP 150, retailer 15%, distributor 6%, primary scheme 4% => RLP 138.65, DLP 130.8; a 10% QPS on 4 cases drops the effective rate to 128. Margins are quoted "on PTD, not MRP" by distributors ("PTD Rs 80, PTR Rs 90, MRP Rs 100 gives you 12.5% margin on PTD" — SpireStock) but brands and retailers quote margin on MRP — the product must store the basis explicitly. Real invoice B shows this in SAP form: MRP/Each 15.00, Rate/Unit 135.43 per case of 12 (=11.29/bottle), Base 94,801 -> Discount 42,801 -> Taxable 51,997 -> 5% tax -> 54,597; i.e. the discount line carries the whole trade margin and scheme.

**Margins by tier and category (ranges from multiple sources; vendor numbers directional).**

| Category                            | Distributor gross margin                                                 | Retailer margin                                                          | Source                                           |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |

Net margin after schemes, damages, credit cost and opex: "2-4%" (Vrikso; SpireStock). Hidden costs "silently consume 2-6% of revenue" (spoilage 0.5-2%, working-capital interest 0.6-1.25%, retailer defaults 0.25-1%, billing errors 0.15-0.5%). Income mix for a Rs 25 lakh/month distributor: base margin 60-70% of income, scheme benefits 15-25%, volume incentives 10-15%, cash discounts 5-10% (SpireStock income article) — **schemes and claims are a third of a distributor's income**, which is why unclaimed schemes hurt so much. AICPDF's June-2026 open letter says "logistics, basic manpower, and secondary transportation alone swallow up to Rs 57 out of every Rs 100" of margin and demands a "halt to forced stock push systems" (Business Today).

## 5. Schemes: taxonomy, invoice representation, GST treatment

Schemes come from the brand (company-funded) or from the distributor's own pocket (rare, tactical). Company schemes split into **primary** (earned on the distributor's purchases) and **secondary** (earned on what the distributor sells to retailers; the distributor "gives a retailer a discount that the company promised, funds it from its own working capital, and then claims it back" — RebateLedger).

| Scheme                                            | Level                | Basis                                              | How it lands                                                                                | Typical numbers                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Primary scheme / trade discount                   | Primary              | % off on purchase invoice                          | On-invoice discount (invoice B "Discount", invoice A "Disc % 8.33")                         | 4% in worked example                                                                                                  |
| QPS (Quantity Purchase Scheme)                    | Primary or retailer  | Slabs of qty/value in a month/quarter              | Post-period credit note; sometimes free cases                                               | Rs 8-12/case; retailer QPS "12 cases -> 3%, 24 -> 5%, 48 -> 7%"; "achieve Rs 50,000 monthly purchase for a 3% rebate" |
| Slab / TOD (turnover discount)                    | Primary              | % of net purchase value stepping by slab           | Credit note (GST or financial)                                                              | 1.0-2.5% quarterly slabs                                                                                              |
| Secondary scheme                                  | Secondary            | Cases billed to retailers, validated from DMS data | Distributor shows it on retailer bill, claims back; "almost always a financial credit note" | Rs 15/case example                                                                                                    |
| Free goods / FOC / "10+1"                         | Secondary or primary | Free qty per qty bought                            | Free-qty column on invoice (invoice E "Free Qty", F "Free"); effective 9.09% not 10%        | 10+1, 12+1                                                                                                            |
| Display / visibility scheme                       | Retailer             | Per verified outlet per month, geo-tagged photo    | Distributor pays retailer, claims reimbursement                                             | Rs 300-1,500/store/month; Rs 5,000/outlet audited                                                                     |
| Cash discount (CD)                                | Secondary            | Prompt payment                                     | "Cash Dis %" column on invoice E                                                            | "2% CD if paid within 3 days", 1-3%                                                                                   |
| Festive/seasonal top-up                           | Primary              | Stacked on base slab                               | Same instrument                                                                             | +1% on base 1.5%                                                                                                      |
| Bill-cut / lines-per-call / new-launch first-bill | Retailer             | Frequency, assortment                              | Points / UPI                                                                                | Rs 100-400/month; Rs 50-200 first bill                                                                                |
| Price protection / rate difference                | Primary              | Brand cuts price or GST rate on stock held         | Claim within 7-15 days of announcement                                                      | Sept-2025 GST 2.0 created a wave of these (ET)                                                                        |

**How schemes appear on real retailer bills.** Invoice E (brand DMS): MRP, Free Qty, Qty, Price/Piece, Net Amt, **Secondary Dis %**, **Cash Dis %**, Taxable, GST%, GST Amt. Invoice F (own software): Qty, Free, MRP, Rate, **Scheme**, Disc Amt, Taxable. So a retailer line needs: qty, free qty, rate, scheme discount (value or %), cash discount, resulting taxable value — with each discount tagged to the scheme id it came from so the claim can be reconstructed.

**GST rules that shape scheme representation.**

- Discounts shown on the invoice at supply reduce taxable value; post-supply discounts need credit notes (Section 34; must be issued/declared by 30 November after the FY end — Terra Insight; SpireStock says 30 September, older rule). CBIC Circular 251/08/2025-GST (12 Sept 2025): recipient need not reverse ITC on financial/commercial credit notes; a manufacturer-directed discount to end customers "must be included in the value of supply"; ordinary post-sale discounts are "price reductions only" and not consideration for a service (EY alert).
- "Buy one get one" / 10+1 on the same invoice is a single supply at a composite price — no ITC reversal (Circular 92/11/2019). Genuinely free samples require ITC reversal (Section 17(5)(h)). Hence FOC lines on the same invoice are the tax-safe way to give free goods.
- A credit note for returns must be at the **original dispatch tax rate** ("a KitKat dispatched August 2025 at 18% returned October 2025 must be credited at 18%, not ... 5%" — Terra Insight) — relevant since biscuits/namkeen/water all changed rate on 22 Sept 2025.

## 6. Claims to the manufacturer — and why distributors lose money

Claim types (SpireStock, FieldAssist, RebateLedger): (1) scheme claims — largest by value, "scheme shortfall (30-40% of claims)"; (2) damage/breakage; (3) expiry returns; (4) promotional/display and merchandiser costs; (5) price protection / rate difference; (6) logistics/transport; plus distributor-defined custom claims (internal incentives).

Lifecycle: incurrence -> accrual -> documentation -> submission -> verification -> settlement (credit note, or adjustment against next invoice / "in-bill") -> reconciliation. Windows quoted: damage "24-48 hours from receipt (GRN remark); formal claim within 7 days"; scheme shortfall "within 15 days of scheme period ending"; expiry "within 30 days of product expiry date" (but most brands require notification 30-90 days **before** expiry — ShelfLifePro); rate difference "within 7-15 days". Settlement time: MNCs 30-60 days, large Indian corporates 20-45, mid-size 15-30, regional 7-21 days (vendor figures).

Why money leaks (multiple sources agree on mechanism; magnitudes are vendor estimates):

- "Distributors using manual tracking miss 30-50% of available scheme claims"; "the largest loss source is claims never raised."
- Rejection causes: "Incomplete documentation (35-40%), filed after deadline (15-20%), incorrect calculation (10-15%), damage not noted on GRN (10-12%), scheme interpretation dispute (8-10%), duplicate claim (5-8%), batch/invoice mismatch (5-7%)."
- Expiry claims approved 40-75%; batch-level tracking pushes approval to 80-95% vs 30-50% with SKU-level tracking (ShelfLifePro; a Coimbatore distributor recovered Rs 2.9L of Rs 4.8L).
- Evidence demanded: batch, mfg/expiry, qty, original purchase invoice ref, timestamped photos with batch/MRP visible, GRN with damage remark, transporter acknowledgement, stock statement, scheme circular, calculation sheet; witnessed destruction for large expiry claims.
- Real-world power asymmetry: a 20-year distributor reports claims that "use to be cleared within two months" delayed 2.5 years, company forcing a "full and final settlement" resignation letter to release Rs 35k cash + Rs 14k claims (LawyersClubIndia); Tata Consumer distributors "beg the depot team" for credit notes (Storyboard18). Claims are also part of the **investment** base in the distributor ROI formula (Marketing Weekly: "Pending claims/incentives: Rs 10,000" counted as capital locked).

## 7. Damage, expiry and returns — per brand policy

- Return categories: expiry, transit damage (1-3% of dispatches), quality complaint, recall, commercial (slow-moving) — SpireStock returns guide.
- Liability split (Terra Insight): transit damage = transporter/3PL if noted on POD/LR at delivery; godown damage = distributor's own risk ("brand issues credit notes only for insurance claims or force-majeure"); primary packaging failure = brand, "credit-noted in full". Hence invoice D (Sneha Logicare LR, 48 packages, receiver signature) is the document on which transit damage must be annotated at unloading — otherwise the claim is dead.
- RTV bands by shelf life: long (detergent, atta) 60-90 days before expiry; medium (biscuits: "Britannia Tiger, Parle-G") 45-60 days; short (juice, chocolate) 30-60 days. Snacks shelf life 90-180 days. Brands "cap the claimable percentage"; some formalise "100% replacement on date-expired stock returned within 30 days, 1-2% standing damage allowance" (Unotags). Founder confirms some brands take nothing back — so policy must be a per-manufacturer (per brand, even per SKU) configuration with: takes back Y/N, band (days before expiry), cap (% of purchases), evidence list, window, settlement mode (credit note / replacement / in-bill).
- Near-expiry bands used in practice: green 60+ days, yellow 30-60, amber 15-30, red <15; liquidation ladders "10% at 45 days, 20% at 30 days, 35% at 15 days, 50% at 7 days"; FEFO cuts write-offs "from 3-5% to 0.5-1.2%" (vendor). Retailer-side returns are collected by salesman/delivery crew and must be split saleable vs damaged at pickup (SalesOn).

## 8. Trade credit norms

- General trade credit: "7-21 days" (SpireStock), "7-30 days" typical; distributor pays the company "on or near delivery" (advance/RTGS for big brands) — so the distributor finances the retailer (Vrikso). Kirana udhaar to consumers is 40-90% of a month's revenue (ShelfLifePro), which is why kiranas pay distributors late.
- Practice at the counter: **bill-to-bill** (pay the previous bill when the next delivery arrives) is the informal norm; the founder's "retailers pay in bulk every 2-3 orders; pending bills sit in a physical file" is the same thing with slack. DSO for distributors "45-60 days" (GetPaidly); receivables 35-60% of monthly revenue at scale vs 15-25% when small, "severe" above 40-50% (retailpos). Aging buckets used: 0-30 / 31-60 / 61-90 / 90+ with supply stoppage at 90+; weekly cadence "Monday: aging report -> call list; Wednesday follow-up; Friday closing" (UdyogBill). Credit-limit rule of thumb: "average monthly purchases x credit days / 30 x 80%" (SpireStock). Salesmen and delivery staff both collect cash; delayed reporting of that cash is a named pain point.
- Cash discount (CD) is the lever to pull payment forward: "1.5% cash discount to kirana stores that pay within 48 hours of delivery" (Nagpur example, SpireStock glossary); CD given at supply reduces taxable value, given later needs a credit note.

## 9. Compliance facts the product must encode (verified against legal/tax sources)

- **GST tax invoice (Rule 46)**: consecutive invoice no., date, supplier GSTIN, recipient name/address and GSTIN if registered, place of supply, HSN per line, description, qty + unit, taxable value, discounts, rate and amount of CGST/SGST or IGST, reverse-charge flag, signature; three copies for goods (original recipient, duplicate transporter, triplicate supplier); issue "before or at the time of removal" (ClearTax). Unregistered retailers (invoices E and F) are billed as **URP/B2C**; name and address mandatory only above Rs 50,000 per invoice, but the product should always print them.
- **HSN digits**: 4 digits up to Rs 5 crore turnover, 6 digits above (SpireStock GST guide; CBIC notification 78/2020 — cite from memory, verify).
- **E-invoicing (IRN/QR)**: mandatory for B2B invoices of suppliers with aggregate turnover > Rs 5 crore in any FY since 2017-18 (ClearTax/IndiaFilings). Retailer bills to unregistered kiranas never need IRN; Tarsun's own turnover will decide whether its B2B bills (registered supermarkets, sub-distributors) need IRN — build the IRP integration as a later module but keep the invoice schema IRN-ready. Inbound: large suppliers' invoices carry IRN + signed QR (B, C); micro suppliers' don't (A).
- **E-way bill**: inter-state > Rs 50,000; **Maharashtra intra-state > Rs 1,00,000** (Notification 15E of 29 June 2018; ClearTax, Busy). Threshold is per consignment/invoice value including tax, excluding exempt goods; multiple consignments on one van can be covered by a consolidated EWB; Part B (vehicle) not required for movement < 50 km between consignor and transporter within the state; validity 1 day per 200 km; penalty Rs 10,000 or tax, whichever higher, plus detention. For a Kalyan distributor almost no retailer bill will cross Rs 1 lakh, but a full Campa truck (invoice B: Rs 54,597 per 700 cases) and stock transfers between godowns can; the inbound invoices already show EWB numbers.
- **FSSAI**: 14-digit licence/registration number mandatory on "cash receipts/purchase invoices/cash memo/bills" (FSSAI order 8 June 2021, effective 1 Oct 2021, "excluding GST e-way bill"; some sources cite a later 1 Jan 2022 date) — print supplier FSSAI on every retailer bill; capture the retailer's FSSAI registration as an optional field.
- **GST rates after 22 Sept 2025 (GST 2.0, Notification 9/2025-CT(Rate))**: namkeen/bhujia (HSN 2106) 5%; potato chips/wafers (2005/1905) 5%; biscuits (1905) 5% (the 18% premium slab removed); plain packaged drinking water (2201) 5% (from 12%/18%); sweetened/flavoured drinks (2202) 40%; plain aerated water 18% (TaxClue). Consistent with the observed invoices (all 5%). Rate is a dated attribute of the HSN, and credit notes use the original invoice's rate.
- **Legal Metrology**: see section 4. Also Rule 24 lets the case count be declared on the outer, which the warehouse app can use to validate case size at GRN.

## 10. Exact data a retailer bill needs (union of Rule 46, FSSAI, Legal Metrology and the three observed formats)

Header: distributor legal name, address, GSTIN, FSSAI no., contact/UPI ID and UPI QR; document type ("Tax Invoice"/"Bill of Supply") and copy label (Original/Duplicate/Triplicate; brand DMS prints "Seller Copy 1/3"); invoice no. (per-series, sequential), date, time, due date/credit days; retailer name, shop address, phone, retailer code, GSTIN if any (else "URP"), FSSAI if known, beat name, salesman name + phone, delivery person/vehicle, order ref (SO no.), place of supply/state code, brand-DMS references when applicable (Buyer ERP Id, Item ERP Id, SO/IN numbers as on invoice E).
Line: item name as retailer knows it, brand, HSN, batch no., expiry/best-before (snacks), **MRP**, UoM shown as pcs with case equivalent, qty, free qty, rate (per piece and per case), scheme name/id and scheme discount, secondary discount %, cash discount %, taxable value, GST % and CGST/SGST (or IGST) amounts, line total.
Footer: subtotal, total discount, taxable total, tax split by rate, round-off, grand total in words; previous outstanding, amount received now, mode (cash/UPI/cheque ref), new balance; returns/credit notes adjusted; e-way bill no. if generated; "Goods once sold..." and brand return-policy text; signature/OTP acknowledgment.

## 11. Glossary

- **Primary / secondary / tertiary sales** — company->distributor / distributor->retailer / retailer->consumer.
- **C&F (CFA)** — carrying & forwarding agent; holds brand stock in a state depot, does not own it.
- **Super stockist (SS)** — buys from company/CFA, sells to several distributors in a district; **sub-stockist / sub-distributor** — buys from a distributor to serve an outlying pocket.
- **RS** — redistribution stockist (HUL's term for distributor). **DB** — distributor (industry shorthand).
- **GT / MT** — general trade (kirana) / modern trade (chains).
- **Beat**, **PJP**, **DSR (daily sales report)** — see section 2. **DSM / DSR / ISR** — distributor's salesman; **TSI / SO / ASM** — company field staff. **PSR** — pre-sales rep (books orders, no stock).
- **PCR / strike rate** — productive calls / calls; **LPC** — lines per call; **ECO** — effectively covered outlets; **bill-cut** — a retailer billed at least once in the period.
- **MRP, PTR/RLP, PTD/DLP, landing price** — see section 4. **Margin on MRP vs on PTD** — two bases; store which.
- **Case / box / outer / inner / pc** — pack hierarchy; **CS1** — SAP UoM code for case; "x 90", "_120" — case size suffixes in item names.
- **Scheme** — any trade promotion; **primary scheme**, **secondary scheme**, **QPS**, **slab/TOD**, **display scheme**, **FOC / free qty / 10+1**, **CD (cash discount)**, **secondary discount** (the discount the distributor passes to the retailer on the company's behalf), **price protection / rate difference**.
- **Claim** — distributor's request to the company to be reimbursed for a scheme, damage, expiry, display or rate difference; settled by **credit note (CN)** (tax CN reduces GST; financial/commercial CN does not), by **in-bill adjustment**, or by free stock.
- **GRN** — goods receipt note (record of what physically arrived vs invoice); **LR / GR** — lorry receipt / goods receipt issued by transporter (invoice D); **POD** — proof of delivery; **RTV** — return to vendor; **L&D** — leakage & damage claim; **SLOB** — slow-moving and obsolete stock; **FEFO** — first-expiry-first-out.
- **Load sheet / loading sheet** — per-trip manifest signed by driver; **van godown** — vehicle treated as a stock location; **EOD settlement** — stock + cash reconciliation at trip end.
- **Bill-to-bill** — retailer pays the previous bill when the next is delivered; **udhaar/khata** — running credit; **DSO** — days sales outstanding; **aging** — 0-30/31-60/61-90/90+.
- **URP** — unregistered person (retailer without GSTIN); **IRN/QR** — e-invoice reference; **EWB** — e-way bill; **HSN** — tariff code; **GST 2.0** — 22 Sept 2025 rate rationalisation.
- **Dumping / forced primary** — company pushing stock beyond secondary demand; **closing stock** — distributor stock at period end (the number companies watch).

## 12. Operational pain points, ranked by frequency (most sources + daily recurrence first), with product implication

Ranking basis: number of independent sources naming the issue, plus whether it recurs per bill/day vs per month. Judgment call, not a survey.

1. **Scheme misapplied or missed at billing** (every bill; 6 sources) — scheme engine evaluates all active schemes per line at billing, stamps scheme ids on lines, never lets a salesman type a free-form discount.
2. **Claims never raised or raised late** (monthly; 5 sources; "30-50% missed") — auto-accrue a claim the moment a company-funded discount/free qty/return is booked; per-brand claim window countdown with WhatsApp nudges.
3. **Claims rejected for missing evidence** (monthly; 4 sources) — claim dossier builder: photos with batch/MRP, GRN remark, invoice refs, calc sheet, exported in the brand's template.
4. **Retailer outstanding sprawl; bills in a physical file** (daily; founder + 5 sources) — per-retailer ledger with aging, bill-to-bill default, credit-limit block at billing, collection on delivery app, UPI QR on every bill.
5. **Book stock vs physical stock mismatch** (daily; 3 sources) — append-only ledger with GRN variance, pick/pack confirmations, van load/unload and damage as explicit movements; cycle counts.
6. **Case vs pieces confusion** (every inbound and outbound document; ground truth invoices) — pack hierarchy per SKU with supplier-specific conversion, invoice extraction that parses "x 90"/"_120"/CS1 and asks the reviewer to confirm.
7. **Three billing systems in parallel because brands mandate their DMS** (daily; ground truth) — import brand-DMS invoices (E) as photos/CSV so the distributor still gets one ledger; never assume our system is the only invoice source.
8. **Forced primary / auto-generated orders / month-end dumping** (monthly; AICPDF/Tata case) — PO-vs-invoice matching on inbound; days-of-stock per SKU visible to the owner before accepting primary; record disputed receipts.
9. **Expiry write-offs because near-expiry was not flagged inside the brand's RTV window** (weekly; 4 sources) — batch/expiry captured at GRN, FEFO picking, alerts at 60/30/15/7 days tuned to the brand's return band.
10. **Van/delivery blind spot: what did the van sell, what is left, where is the cash** (daily; founder wants GPS; 4 sources) — trip object with load sheet, per-stop status, on-vehicle invoicing, EOD stock and cash close.
11. **End-of-trip cash reconciliation and cash leakage** (daily; 3 sources) — expected-vs-collected per trip, deposit confirmation by manager, variance log.
12. **Short/partial delivery disputes hold up payment** (daily; 3 sources) — partial-delivery state producing a corrected invoice, OTP/photo POD.
13. **Salesman attrition destroys beat knowledge** (quarterly; 2 sources but severe) — retailer master, outlet class and history owned by the tenant, not the rep; new rep inherits beats with last-order context.
14. **Beats built on habit, stale outlet master, no visit proof** (weekly; 4 sources) — beat/PJP objects, GPS check-in, coverage vs universe, outlet class-driven frequency.
15. **Secondary data nobody trusts; brand reports take 3-5 days a month** (monthly; 3 sources) — brand-wise secondary sales/closing stock export in the formats brands ask for.
16. **Credit-note failures and GST Section 34 deadlines** (monthly) — credit notes linked to original invoice and its original tax rate; deadline tracking per FY.
17. **GST/MRP rate change on stock in hand** (episodic but large — Sept 2025) — batch-level MRP and dated HSN rates; price-protection claim generator.
18. **Transit damage not noted at unloading on the LR** (per inbound truck) — LR as a document type with mandatory damage remark + photos before GRN close.
19. **Ambiguous scheme stacking** (per scheme circular) — scheme definition carries stacking rule and "final" flag (already decided in CONTEXT).
20. **Cash discount given but payment still late** (per bill) — CD as a conditional discount realised only on payment within window; else auto-reversal via credit note logic.
21. **Role leak: salesman sees purchase price/margin** (Vyapar failure in CONTEXT) — field-level authorisation enforced server-side; separate price-list views.
22. **FOC/free-goods accounting wrong (10+1 booked as 10%)** (per scheme) — compute effective scheme cost on units delivered; ITC handling for true samples.
23. **Delivery windows and multi-trip sequencing** (daily) — trip planner with stop windows; load last-stop-first order.
24. **E-way bill for high-value inbound and inter-godown moves** (weekly) — EWB fields on inbound docs; threshold check (Rs 1 lakh MH intra-state) on any outbound consignment.
25. **FSSAI number missing on bills; unregistered retailers** (per bill) — mandatory FSSAI on invoice template; URP handling.
26. **Retailers order in pieces below case MOQ; broken cases** (per order) — sell-UoM vs stock-UoM; loose-piece stock balance per SKU.
27. **Doorstep price disputes ("salesman promised a scheme")** (daily) — scheme shown to retailer in-app at order time; order confirmation shared on WhatsApp with prices.
28. **Saleable vs damaged returns mixed up** (per trip) — return reason codes at pickup, separate stock buckets, brand-claim eligibility flag.
29. **Multiple MRPs of the same SKU in stock** (weekly) — stock lots keyed by batch+MRP; bill shows the MRP of the lot picked.
30. **Owner has no live view; learns at month end** (daily) — the owner dashboard already decided; feed it from the ledgers above rather than from DSR compilation.

## 13. Flags vs decisions already made in CONTEXT (recommended changes, with reasons)

- Van sales: decide **yes** (section 3). Add "vehicle/trip as a stock location" to the inventory model and "invoice at delivery" to the delivery app. Otherwise the Campa thermal-slip flow (invoice G) has no home.
- Add a **Claim** aggregate (per manufacturer, per type, with window, evidence, settlement) and a **Scheme** master that records funding source (company vs distributor) and claimability. Today CONTEXT treats schemes as pricing-engine inputs only; a third of distributor income depends on claiming them back.
- Add **LR/GRN variance and damage-at-receipt** to the manager app's inbound flow; CONTEXT has LR as a document type but not the damage annotation that decides claim liability.
- Batch **and MRP** must be attributes of a stock lot (CONTEXT has batch only). Sept-2025 rate change and routine MRP revisions make MRP-per-lot mandatory for correct bills and price-protection claims.
- Store margin basis (on MRP vs on PTD) and pack conversions per supplier, not just per SKU, because the same "Campa 1L" arrives as CS1 x 12 from Reliance but may be bought loose from a sub-distributor.
- FSSAI number and UPI QR should be template-mandatory on the retailer bill (CONTEXT has UPI QR; FSSAI is new).
