Four ways the apps could look

Same product, same screens, same data — four visual directions. Every one shows the owner dashboard on the left as it appears on an office PC, and a salesperson's shop screen on the right as it appears on a phone in the field. The numbers are your real demo data: Tarsun Enterprises, Station Road beat, 68 invoices worth ₹12,47,075.

Pick the one that feels right and I build all six apps in it. You are choosing character, not layout — where things sit is fixed by how the work is done.

All four obey the same rulesSet by research into what actually breaks field apps in India.
Readable in Kalyan sun7:1 text contrast minimum. Light background always, on phones.
Big enough for a thumb11 mm targets, not the 7 mm the phone makers suggest.
Works with wet handsMonsoon. Taps only, no swipe-to-confirm anywhere.
Money never misreadFigures align in columns, one size larger than the words.
A

Ledger

warm paper, ink, hairlines

The look of a well-kept accounts book. A warm off-white page rather than the cold grey every dashboard uses, near-black text, thin rules instead of boxes, and a single deep teal for anything you can act on. Dense and quiet. Nothing decorative anywhere. Colour appears only when it means something: green paid, amber due, red overdue.

How to choose

Look at the phone screens first. Six people will use those all day; you will use the desk screen. Then reply with a single letter.

  1. A, Ledger — if you want it to feel like the books, and to show the most information.
  2. B, Instrument — if you want it to look unmistakably modern and precise.
  3. C, Panel — if you want it to feel easy and familiar to a new employee.
  4. D, Signal — if readability in sunlight and at speed matters more than density.

You can also mix: for example "D on the phones, A on the desk", or "B but warmer". Nothing here is final. Once you pick, I build the full component set in that direction and every one of the six apps uses it.
