# Purpose

This document identifies operational pain points observed during distributor research and maps them to their business impact, root causes, and the solution in Distribution OS. It serves as the primary justification for product features and helps prioritize development based on customer value rather than assumptions.

Each pain point names its **Solution in Distribution OS** — the module, procedure or table that answers it. The product name is **Distribution OS**, which is also the name of the **single app** the six business roles share — it becomes the right app for whoever signs in — alongside a separate internal admin console.

**Analysis framework.** Each pain point is analyzed as: Current Situation · Root Cause · Business Impact · Severity · Frequency · Affected Personas · **Solution in Distribution OS** · Related Modules.

# Pain Point 1 – Manual Order Entry

**Current situation.** Orders arrive through phone calls and WhatsApp messages. A data entry operator manually enters every order into billing software to generate invoices. **Root cause.** No digital order capture · multiple communication channels · no structured order format.

**Business impact.** High manual effort · slow processing · typing errors · duplicate work · delayed dispatch. **Severity:** Critical · **Frequency:** Daily

**Affected personas.** Sales Representative, Owner, Manager. The **Data Entry Operator persona is designed away** rather than supported: orders are captured by the rep or the shop and the invoice is generated by the system at pack time, so no re-keying step remains for an operator to own.

**Solution in Distribution OS.**

- **Mobile order capture** — `orders.create`, `orders.setLines`, `orders.submit`, `orders.repeatLast`, priced on the device by the pricing engine before submit.
- **Every order carries its channel** — `source` = `salesperson` | `retailer_app` | `van_sale` | `phone` | `whatsapp`, so the channel mix is measurable from day one.
- **Web order entry** for genuine phone orders — the manager keys them in on the manager's screens as `source = phone`.
- **The invoice is never typed** — it is derived from the confirmed pack (`warehouse.packs.confirm` → billing issues the GST invoice). Zero re-keying is structural, not a matter of discipline.
- **WhatsApp free text and voice capture are part of version 1**: text or speech is parsed against the shop's own SKU history into a **draft** order that a human always confirms.

WhatsApp and voice intake run in the `ai` module, with inbound message capture in notifications. **Related modules:** orders, pricing, ai, notifications.

# Pain Point 2 – WhatsApp-Based Order Management

**Current situation.** Sales representatives send retailer orders as free-text messages in WhatsApp groups. **Root cause.** No centralized order management system.

**Business impact.** Missed orders · duplicate orders · difficult searching · no tracking · no order history. **Severity:** Critical · **Frequency:** Daily

**Solution in Distribution OS.** Structured order creation on the sales screens (`orders.create` / `setLines` / `submit`), searchable history through `orders.list` filtered by shop, salesperson, state and date range, and `pricing.bargains.request` for a price the rep cannot give alone. The product does not _replace_ WhatsApp; it _absorbs_ it. Shops and reps keep the channel they already use; a free-text message becomes a draft order through the `ai` intake parser, always human-confirmed before it counts. Removing WhatsApp was never realistic; making it structured is.

**Related modules:** orders, notifications, ai.

# Pain Point 3 – Invoice Becomes the Entire Workflow

**Current situation.** A single printed invoice is used as bill, picking list, delivery note, payment tracker and delivery confirmation.

**Business impact.** If the invoice is lost or damaged, operational information is lost. There is no real-time status visibility. **Severity:** Critical · **Frequency:** Daily

**Solution in Distribution OS.** Each job the paper invoice used to do is its own record: `picklists` and `pick_lines` (picking), `pack_confirmations` (packing), `load_sheets` and `delivery_challans` (loading and dispatch), `trip_stops` and `deliveries` with `pod_evidence` (delivery confirmation), `receipts` and `allocations` (payment tracking). The invoice keeps exactly one job — the legal financial document. It is **issued at pack** by the warehouse, it is **immutable once issued**, corrections are credit or debit notes, and a cancelled invoice keeps its number. Enforced by database triggers, not by convention.

**Related modules:** warehouse, billing, delivery, receivables.

# Pain Point 4 – No Order Tracking

**Current situation.** Once an invoice is printed, management has no visibility into order progress: has the warehouse picked it, is it loaded, is it on the vehicle, is it delivered? **Severity:** Critical · **Frequency:** Daily

**Solution in Distribution OS.** Order lifecycle tracking with defined states — a state machine is the only thing permitted to move a state column:

`draft` → `submitted` → `confirmed` → `picking` → `packed` → `dispatched` → `delivered` | `partially_delivered` → `closed`

| Step often expected | In the product                                                                             |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Allocated           | Not a state — stock reservation is a side effect of `confirmed`                            |
| Loaded              | Not an order state — `warehouse.loadSheets.confirm`, which moves the order to `dispatched` |
| Out for Delivery    | Not an order state — the trip is `active`, the stop is `started` / `arrived`               |

Two further lifecycles run alongside the order: **trip** (`planned` → `loading` → `active` → `closing` → `settled` | `settled_with_variance`) with **stop** (`pending` → `started` → `arrived` → `delivered` | `partial` | `failed`), and **invoice** (`draft` → `issued` → `partially_paid` → `paid`; "overdue" is computed, never stored). Every transition is written to `order_state_transitions` with the actor and device id, so "who moved it and when" is answerable for any order.

**Related modules:** orders, warehouse, delivery, billing.

# Pain Point 5 – Shop Location Problems

**Current situation.** Drivers receive a photograph or vague description of the shop location.

**Business impact.** Delayed deliveries · increased calls · wrong locations · reduced productivity. **Severity:** High · **Frequency:** Daily

**Solution in Distribution OS.** Coordinates on the shop record (`retailers.upsert` with lat/lng), a geo-tag captured at every sales check-in (`visits`) and every delivery arrival (`trip_stops.arrivedLat/Lng`), and a hand-off to the phone's own maps app from `delivery.stops.next`. There is **no "verified location" flag**. Verification is accumulated evidence — the rep's check-in geo-tag and the driver's arrival geo-tag against the stored coordinate. The geo-tag is **evidence, never a block**: a rep with weak GPS can still check in and take the order. The shop is shown an ETA, never a live coordinate of the vehicle.

**Related modules:** retailers, delivery.

# Pain Point 6 – Payment Tracking

**Current situation.** Drivers write payment status on paper invoices; outstanding balances are updated manually after returning to the office.

**Business impact.** Delayed financial visibility · reconciliation effort · lost payment records. **Severity:** Critical · **Frequency:** Daily

**Solution in Distribution OS.** `delivery.collections.record` writes, in one transaction: a receipt row, a **balanced double-entry journal** (balance enforced by a database trigger at commit), and allocation against the oldest open bill first unless the shop tags a specific invoice. Cash, UPI with UTR and cheque are supported, with `receipts.deposit` and `receipts.bounce` covering the cheque life cycle including bank charges and the bill reopening. **Who may take money:** the **delivery crew** at the door, the **shop itself** paying online in the app, or the **desk** (owner, manager, accountant) for money received at the office. The **salesperson never records a receipt** — the sales service has no receipt endpoint and the permission matrix never grants one. This is a non-negotiable, tested per endpoint per role. Offline is first-class: collections queue through `sync.upload`, which never answers a 4xx — rejections come back as durable, visible errors rather than lost payments.

Online payment by the shop is `receivables.payments.initiate`. **Related modules:** delivery, receivables, billing, platform (sync).

# Pain Point 7 – Inventory Visibility

**Current situation.** Inventory is updated only after billing or manual entry; warehouse staff rely on experience rather than accurate system data.

**Business impact.** Stock shortages · overselling · incorrect commitments. **Severity:** High · **Frequency:** Daily

**Solution in Distribution OS.** An **append-only `stock_ledger`**: a row is written at goods receipt (`procurement.grns.post`), reservation (`orders.confirm`), pick and pack (`warehouse.packs.confirm`), load-out (godown → vehicle), return (a credit note restocks), and every adjustment, transfer or cycle count (`inventory.*`). Balances are derived, never edited; every mutation carries an idempotency key, so a retried phone request cannot double-count stock. Available-to-promise comes from the `sellable_stock` view, so the rep's device shows what can actually be sold — on hand minus already reserved — rather than a book figure.

**Related modules:** inventory, procurement, warehouse, orders.

# Pain Point 8 – Warehouse Operations

**Current situation.** Picking depends on staff familiarity with product locations. No rack or bin management exists.

**Business impact.** Slow picking · picking errors · staff dependency. **Severity:** Medium · **Frequency:** Daily

**Solution in Distribution OS.** Consolidated pick lists by SKU across the wave, **FEFO lot suggestion** (first expiry, first out) so the picker is told which batch to take, per-line short-pack reasons, and a pack confirmation that becomes the invoice. Stock locations exist as `warehouse`, `vehicle`, `damaged`, `in_transit` and `customer`. There is **no rack, bin or zone model and no barcode scanning** in version 1. The only QR the product reads is the supplier's e-invoice QR on an inbound bill. A godown of this size gains more from FEFO and short-pack discipline than from a bin hierarchy nobody maintains.

Racks, bins, zones and barcode scanning are not available yet. **Related modules:** warehouse, inventory.

# Pain Point 9 – Delivery Management

**Current situation.** Drivers receive printed invoices without optimized routes or live tracking.

**Business impact.** Longer delivery times · difficult monitoring · no ETA visibility. **Severity:** High · **Frequency:** Daily

**Solution in Distribution OS.** Trips with sequenced stops, a manager-approved load-out (the **manager gives the approval from the manager's screens** — it is not a PIN typed on the warehouse phone), live vehicle positions (`delivery.vehicles.positions` fed by `/gps/points`), proof of delivery by photo, signature or OTP, partial and failed outcomes with reasons, returns as credit notes, van sales from vehicle stock, and end-of-day settlement with a cash variance the owner must clear. **GPS is trip-scoped:** points are captured during a trip only, under a recorded consent (`location_consents`), retained 90 days. The shop sees an ETA, never a coordinate. **Route optimisation is part of version 1**: stop sequencing by distance and time window, with the driver always free to override the suggested order.

Route sequencing runs in the `ai` module. **Related modules:** delivery, ai, billing, receivables.

# Pain Point 10 – Limited Business Insights

**Current situation.** Management receives end-of-day reports but lacks operational visibility throughout the day.

**Business impact.** Slow decisions · reactive management · missed opportunities. **Severity:** High · **Frequency:** Daily

**Solution in Distribution OS.** Every operational register is already live — open orders, outstanding by ageing bucket, active trips, the billing queue — so today's answer is a list, not a report run at night. On top of that, the reporting module adds the **owner dashboard** (a rolling summary refreshed every fifteen minutes), drill-down registers (rep productivity, delivery performance, fill rate, stock value, scheme spend), and alerts through notifications (dues reminders, deliveries due today). **Owner graphs:** the owner's screens show growth and performance as charts wherever possible, which is why reporting serves chart-ready series — month grain, year on year, grouped by beat — rather than only tables.

**Related modules:** reporting, notifications.

# Pain Point 11 – Customer Credit Visibility

**Current situation.** Sales representatives often do not know a retailer's outstanding balance or credit limit when taking new orders.

**Business impact.** Excessive credit exposure · delayed collections · credit policy violations. **Severity:** High · **Frequency:** Daily

**Solution in Distribution OS.** The rep sees the shop's outstanding, ageing and ledger (`receivables.outstanding.get`, `receivables.ledger.get`, scoped to their own beats), and the order screen runs `receivables.creditCheck` **before** submit: inside the limits the order auto-confirms, over the limit it becomes an approval request the owner or manager clears. Credit terms are set by the back office only (`retailers.setCredit` — limit, open-bill count, days, mode). The accountant is the money desk and may record receipts, deposits, cheque bounces and write-offs and may read and export everything, but may **not** set credit limits, prices, schemes, approvals or settings. **The rep sees the dues and never collects them** — see Pain Point 6. Visibility and collection are deliberately separated.

**Related modules:** receivables, retailers, orders, pricing.

# Pain Point 12 – Fragmented Systems

**Current situation.** Different tasks are handled across billing software, WhatsApp, phone calls, Excel and paper.

**Business impact.** Duplicate data · inconsistent information · difficult auditing. **Severity:** Critical · **Frequency:** Daily

**Solution in Distribution OS.** One database, one API contract, one set of master data: a globally curated catalogue of manufacturers, products and variants with a per-distributor overlay; a global shop identity with per-distributor links, so a shop buying from three distributors is one identity with three relationships and one login. Backend modules never read each other's tables — they talk through published services and an outbox — which is what keeps the audit trail whole. **Legacy systems coexist rather than being switched off**: a **generic importer** (upload → preview → map columns → save the profile → dry run → commit) takes data from TradeEzee, Marg, Busy, Tally, FieldAssist or plain Excel; Tally export keeps the accountant's existing workflow; a sale already billed in a brand's DMS is imported and linked, **never re-invoiced**.

The generic importer, FieldAssist import and Tally export are in the `integrations` module. **Related modules:** platform (tenancy), catalog, retailers, integrations.

# Product Design Implications

Each implication below is a property of the product:

- **Mobile-first workflows for field users.** One app for the six business roles, which becomes the right app after sign-in, on the web and Android; the iOS app is built from the same code and is not released yet. Sales and Delivery work offline; an offline upload never returns an error that loses work.
- **Workflow-based processing instead of paper-driven operations.** State columns move only through coded state machines; the invoice is one record among many, not the workflow itself.
- **Real-time status tracking across all operational stages,** with every transition attributed to an actor and a device.
- **Shared master data to eliminate duplicate entry** — one catalogue, one shop identity across distributors.
- **Configurable where it should be, fixed where it must be.** Configurable per distributor: branding and logo, numbering series, credit modes and limits, schemes, settlement tolerance, proof-of-delivery policy, feature flags. Deliberately **not** configurable: state machines, the seven roles, approval kinds and the per-endpoint permission matrix — a distributor cannot invent a workflow state or grant a salesperson the ability to collect cash.
- **White-labelled by default.** The distributor's own name and logo appear inside the app and on every printed document; Distribution OS branding appears only on the sign-in screen.
- **AI ships in the product**, under the "AI drafts, a human commits" guardrail above.
- **Cost is invisible by construction.** Purchase cost, landed cost and margin are unreadable by the salesperson, warehouse, delivery and retailer roles — enforced by database row-level security with tests, not by hiding a field in the interface.

# Success Criteria

Distribution OS should demonstrably reduce or eliminate these pain points. Each criterion is measurable from data the system already records; the Success Metrics page names the table or procedure behind each.

- **Minimizing manual data entry** — orders captured digitally as a share of all orders (`sales_orders.source`); supplier bill lines committed without an edit (`docint`).
- **Increasing operational visibility** — time from order submitted to invoice issued (`order_state_transitions`); share of the day's orders visible in a live register rather than an end-of-day report.
- **Improving inventory accuracy** — counted versus expected at cycle count (`cycle_count_lines`); short-pack rate.
- **Reducing delivery delays** — on-time rate from stop ETA versus arrival; failed-delivery rate and reason mix.
- **Accelerating payment reconciliation** — average days to allocate a receipt against a bill; cash variance at trip settlement.
- **Enabling informed, data-driven decision-making** — owner dashboard adoption and the ageing profile trend, both served by the `reporting` module.
